# Customer Base Overview and Acquisition

- **Investigate the causes of the acquisition decline.**  
  The 54% reduction in acquisition between 2010 and 2011 is large enough to require management attention. The business should review historical marketing activity, acquisition channels, customer geography, and market conditions to determine whether the decline resulted from lower campaign activity, weaker demand, or market saturation.

- **Replicate successful acquisition strategies and prepare for seasonal demand.**  
  March 2010 and the recurring September–October peak should be reviewed for successful campaigns, promotions, and product ranges. Proven acquisition tactics should be documented and reused, while marketing and sales activities should begin before the autumn peak.

- **Balance customer acquisition with customer retention.**  
  As acquisition slows, protecting and expanding value from existing customers becomes increasingly important. Resources should therefore be allocated not only to acquiring new customers but also to increasing repeat purchases and long-term customer value.

- **Differentiate customer management by customer value.**  
  The high AOV suggests that wholesale or bulk purchasers significantly influence business performance. High-value customers should receive dedicated services and incentives, while lower-value customers may respond better to automated retention campaigns.

# Customer Engagement

- **Implement a structured onboarding and second-purchase campaign.**  
  Target first-time customers between **60 and 90 days** after their initial order using reminder emails, personalized offers, and product recommendations to increase repeat purchases and reduce early customer loss.

- **Develop a loyalty programme.**  
  Reward loyal and power customers with exclusive discounts, priority service, or loyalty benefits while encouraging repeat customers to move into higher-value segments.

- **Prepare proactively for peak demand.**  
  Increase inventory, marketing activity, and sales outreach before the September–November buying season to maximize seasonal revenue opportunities.

- **Align operational resources with purchasing behaviour.**  
  Allocate additional customer support and order-processing capacity on high-demand weekdays, particularly **Thursdays**.

# Customer Lifetime, Retention and Cohort Analysis

- **Prioritize retention of at-risk customers.**  
  Customers in the **60–120 day** inactivity window should receive targeted retention campaigns, personalized promotions, or reminder emails before they transition into the churned segment.

- **Implement structured win-back campaigns.**  
  Nearly half of the customer base is classified as churned. Re-engagement campaigns with tailored offers or seasonal promotions may recover a portion of these previously valuable customers at a lower cost than acquiring new ones.

- **Monitor cohort retention as a core business KPI.**  
  Rather than focusing solely on customer acquisition, regularly evaluate cohort retention to measure whether marketing initiatives and customer experience improvements strengthen long-term customer relationships.

# Customer Segmentation

- **Prioritize Champions, Loyal, and High-value customers.**  
  Provide exclusive benefits, priority service, personalized offers, and loyalty rewards to protect the customers who generate the largest share of business revenue.

- **Develop customer migration strategies between segments.**  
  Encourage customers to progress from New to Loyal and eventually Champion through personalized communication, loyalty programmes, and repeat-purchase incentives.

- **Allocate marketing resources according to customer value.**  
  Invest more heavily in retaining high-value customer segments while using automated, lower-cost campaigns for lower-value customers to maximize marketing effectiveness.

# Historical Customer Lifetime Value

- **Protect VIP and High-CLV customers.**  
  Provide dedicated account management, exclusive offers, volume incentives, and personalized services to retain the customers who contribute the greatest revenue.

- **Identify customers with the potential to move into higher-value segments.**  
  Target medium-value customers with cross-selling, upselling, and loyalty programmes designed to increase purchase frequency and long-term customer value.

- **Monitor Historical CLV as a strategic KPI.**  
  Track changes in customer value over time to evaluate whether retention initiatives successfully increase the number of high-value customers and reduce dependence on a very small group of VIP customers.
